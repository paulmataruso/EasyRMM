# Notes for slice N3 (ADR-0053 slice 3: channels, the sender, notices, the address policy), for the integrator

## Migration

`server/migrations/00074_notification_channels.sql` (renumber after the last
integrated migration). It goes down and up (tested).

## Changelog lines

* Notification channels: an organization's administrators (and super
  administrators, for the platform's own) add email and signed generic
  webhook channels, test them, show a webhook's address and signing secret
  again, replace the secret, turn channels off and delete them. Platform
  channels are shared with organizations one by one (ADR-0053 slice 3).
* The sender: one worker per server, a lease on every delivery, retries from
  a minute to an hour, given up after 24 hours, quotas of 600 messages an
  hour per organization, a circuit for the mail server, the delivery log.
* The address policy for organizations' webhooks: loopback, link-local,
  cloud metadata, the server itself and reserved ranges are refused, private
  and local networks only once a super administrator approves them per
  channel, checked on the address dialled and pinned to it, no redirects, no
  proxy, HTTPS only.
* Notices for a failing channel, messages not sent, a quota reached and a
  destination waiting for approval, with a journal; Settings > Notifications
  and an organization's Notifications tab.
* New permission `notification.manage` (Organization Administrators, from
  organization-wide grants only, and super administrators).

## Privacy

Recipients' email addresses are stored in `notification_channel_details`
(organization-scoped; never shown to another organization). Webhook URLs and
signing secrets are sealed with `EASYRMM_SECRET_KEY`; the audit log records the
host and port and that a secret was shown, never the URL or the secret. The
delivery log keeps each recipient's address and outcome for 30 days; the mail
server's own reply is shown to super administrators only.

## Dev scope

Every automatic send checks `devscope.Allowed` for the organization charged
and every organization the delivery is about (an out-of-scope delivery is
dropped, never sent), and no notice is raised for an organization outside the
scope. A person's own test is not automatic and is not held back. In slice 3
no automatic delivery is queued yet (rules arrive in slice 4); the guard is in
the sender so that slice 4 cannot bypass it.
